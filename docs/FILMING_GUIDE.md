# How to film a jump for LANDR

The quality of your video decides the quality of the numbers. Five minutes of
setup makes a big difference. Aim for a **drop-vertical jump** (the standard ACL
screening test), but a simple two-foot jump-and-land works for a first try.

## Camera setup
- **Face the camera (FRONT view).** This is the view that shows the knees caving
  inward (valgus) — the most important signal. (A side view is better for knee
  *bend*; for your first try, front view is the priority.)
- **Phone steady and level.** Prop it on a tripod, a stack of books, or a shelf.
  Don't hand-hold it.
- **Height ≈ hip height.** Roughly waist-high, pointing straight ahead (not tilted
  down).
- **Distance ≈ 3–4 m (10–13 ft).** The athlete's **whole body must stay in frame**
  — head to feet — for the entire jump and landing.
- **Orientation:** landscape (sideways) is fine; portrait works too. Just keep the
  full body visible.

## Lighting & clothing
- Bright, even light. Avoid backlighting (don't stand in front of a window).
- Plain, uncluttered background.
- **Fitted clothing; shorts if possible** so the knees and hips are clearly
  visible. Baggy clothes confuse the tracking.
- Only **one person** in the frame.

## The movement (drop-vertical jump)
1. Stand on a sturdy box/step about **30 cm (12 in)** high.
2. **Drop straight down** off the box onto **both feet** at once (don't jump up off
   the box — just drop).
3. **Immediately jump straight up** as high as you can.
4. Land softly.

No box? Just do a controlled **two-foot jump up and land** facing the camera.

## Clip length
- Keep it **short: 3–5 seconds, one rep.**
- Start recording a moment **before** the drop and stop **after** you've landed and
  steadied. Trim extra footage if you can.

## Quick quality check before analyzing
- ✅ Whole body visible the entire time
- ✅ Facing the camera
- ✅ Knees clearly visible (not hidden by baggy clothing)
- ✅ Steady camera, good light
- ✅ One clean rep, ~3–5 seconds

## What to expect
This is a single-camera estimate, so treat the numbers as a **rough screen**, not a
lab measurement — especially knee-bend depth, which a front view under-reads. Valgus
(knees caving in) is the most reliable signal from the front. For best accuracy
later, film a second clip from the **side** too.
